# Personal design judgment

Make fewer, better interface decisions. A playful product may be expressive, a
professional tool dense, and a marketing page editorial or image-led. The constant
is intentional composition, not one visual style. Concrete colors, fonts, radii,
spacing, components and brand rules come from the project.

## Composition and hierarchy

Begin with primary content, scale, secondary relationships and whitespace rhythm;
add containers only when needed. Typography, alignment and proximity carry
structure. Give dominant and supporting content different weight. Centering is a
choice, not a default or prohibition. Editorial asymmetry, image-led layouts,
structured density and restrained utility are equally valid.

Choose one recognizable spatial idea, not several trends or equally weighted
modules. Large type must earn its space: avoid automatic 80–120px marketing
headlines, dramatic line breaks and repeated eyebrow/headline pairs. Product
headings can stay compact. Prefer a few distinct type levels; weight alone cannot
create hierarchy. Keep paragraph measures comfortable and supporting text readable.

Whitespace separates, groups or emphasizes. Give the focal area room rather than
spreading an application out. Dense professional tools optimize scanning and
action speed. Quiet metadata must retain useful contrast.

## Color, material and imagery

Use color for identity, action, state or one focal point. Without a project palette,
begin with coherent neutrals and one useful accent. Do not distribute accents
evenly for decoration. Pastel, beige and monochrome do not automatically mean
premium; a digital product does not imply a purple-blue gradient.

Try spacing, alignment and tonal separation before borders and shadows. Cards
belong to distinct actionable, selectable, movable or stateful objects, not every
conceptual group. Repeated rounded icon/title/description boxes signal weak
hierarchy. Remove wrappers whose boundaries add no understanding. Shadows explain
actual layering, such as menus and overlays.

Radius follows product language and object purpose. Large radii are not inherently
modern; pills fit tags, filters and some compact controls, not every action. If
everything is soft, pastel, bordered, floating and shadowed, remove treatments.

Use meaningful imagery as composition: scale, crop, negative space, edges and
direction. It need not live in a rounded box. Prefer relevant existing assets over
stock filler. Do not fabricate metrics, testimonials or customer claims for polish.

## Components, actions and states

Reuse project equivalents, primitives and tokens. A library supplies implementation
material; its defaults are not finished design. Do not build a parallel system for
one screen or hide different compositions behind booleans. Abstract actual
repetition and shared behavior, not hypothetical reuse.

Give each local context one obvious primary action, quieter secondary actions and
concise labels. Icons add recognition, not decoration. Navigation orients without
competing. Keep internal phases, tools and agent machinery out of product copy
unless they support a real user decision. Errors explain recovery; copy is direct
and useful rather than corporate, robotic or falsely enthusiastic.

Design reachable states: relevant focus, loading, empty, error, success, overflow
and long content. Use semantic elements, labels, keyboard support, visible focus,
readable contrast and sensible hit targets from the beginning. Accessibility is
part of the component, not a later visual treatment.

Mobile needs deliberate priorities, navigation, primary-action placement, image
crop, type scale and density. Do not blindly stack desktop columns or retain its
whitespace. Motion explains state, continuity, feedback or space; detailed motion
decisions belong to the motion module.

## Correct the cause

| Symptom | Highest-impact correction |
| --- | --- |
| Generic | Remove repeated decorative patterns; strengthen alignment, scale, typography or imagery. |
| Cheap | Remove effects, excessive radius and weak borders; correct alignment, spacing and palette precision. |
| Busy | Reduce competing actions, colors and containers; quiet metadata and protect the focal point. |
| Empty | Strengthen primary scale and meaningful density; improve imagery or type instead of filler cards. |
| Sterile | Introduce one expressive choice in typography, composition, imagery or interaction. |
| Dull | Strengthen focal point, imagery, contrast and typography before adding an accent. |
| Noisy | Reduce accent colors and decorative treatment; restore one clear emphasis. |

Judge hierarchy, composition, typography, density and genericness before polishing
alignment, baselines, borders, wrapping and exact spacing. This is an order, not
six mandatory reviews. Technical correctness alone does not establish visual
quality. Finished design has clear hierarchy, intentional composition, coherent
rhythm, deliberate responsiveness and respect for the product. User visual
feedback decides subjective disagreements.
